# Separable effects hazard longitudinal TMLE

This study validates `LTMLE(survival_model="hazard", targeting="one_step")` in
`cleverly.longitudinal` with `Separable` regimens beside static arms: the risk of relapse and of
death when the part of the treatment acting on relapse and the part acting on death take
different levels. Stensrud, Young, Didelez, Robins and Hernán (2022) define the estimand and
identify it by their formula (8), with censoring by (10), counting death first within a time
step. The fit targets the death hazard and the relapse hazard among the death-free apart, with
one coefficient per factor and target. The [separable effects](../longitudinal-tmle.md#separable-effects)
section states the estimator, its conditions and its exact-law checks.

No separable-effects TMLE implementation was located, so
no canonical implementation is compared. The study rests on exact truths and on
repeated-sampling properties.

## What was fitted

| scenario | law, engine and estimands | folds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `in_sample` | a binary baseline covariate, a three-level treatment held over three nodes, censoring, death and relapse. Hazard `LTMLE` with the one-step factor-wise targeting; the risk of each cause at $t = 1, 2, 3$ under `a0`, `a1`, `sep10` and `sep01`, and the contrasts against `a0` | one |
| `cross_fitted` | the same law, engine and estimands. The initial hazards come from the other fold, so the targeting starts away from the truth | two |

`sep10` sets the relapse component to 1 and the death component to 0, and `sep01` the reverse.
The contrast `sep10` against `a0` is the separable direct effect on relapse. The contrast `sep10`
against `a0` for death at $t = 1$ is zero on every sample and is not reported, so each scenario
reports 41 parameters.

The law is in `tests/studies/separable_effects_law.py`, built on
`tests/discrete_law_point_survival.py`. Each truth is identification (8) written as quotients of
cell masses, and each efficiency bound is the exact efficient influence curve, a complex-step
Gateaux derivative. Every nuisance is a saturated cell mean, the nonparametric model on this
support. The cumulative bounds, 0.001, never bind on this law.

In 2.0% of the cross-fitted fits (165 of 8,400) the targeting stopped short of its tolerance,
$10^{-10}$, at a relative score near $10^{-3}$, and warned. The study keeps these fits, as the
shipped fit returns them, and `convergence.csv` records every fit. No in-sample fit stopped
short.

## Limits

| limit | what it means for use |
| --- | --- |
| saturated cell means at every slot | the study does not test a flexible learner. The claim rests on the finite-dimensional class |
| one discrete law at n = 16,000 | smaller samples and other laws are not claimed |
| two causes and baseline covariates only | covariates after baseline are refused; more causes are not measured |
| death counted first within a time step | the other order is a different estimand and is not measured |
| robustness cells on the engine with the exact mechanism | `LTMLE` with one wrong hazard learner is not measured |
| 2.0% of cross-fitted fits stop short of the tolerance | they warn and are kept; their coverage is part of every cross-fitted row |
| no comparator | no separable-effects TMLE implementation was located |
| no weights, clusters or missing outcomes | each is a separate composition and reads experimental |
