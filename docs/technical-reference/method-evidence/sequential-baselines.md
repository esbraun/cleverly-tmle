# Sequential g-computation and IPW

This study validates `SequentialGComputation` (ICE) and `SequentialIPW` (Hajek and Horvitz-Thompson) with `GLM`
nuisances. The estimators fit a parametric regression or mechanism at each node and
report an influence-curve interval from the stacked estimating equations. The section
[sequential g-computation and IPW](../sequential-baselines.md) states the estimators, their
refusals and their exact-law witnesses. The study reads each estimate as an estimate of a known
population value, and it reads each reported standard error against the sampling spread.

**This page is Python property evidence.** No canonical implementation is compared in this study.
The R comparator parity is fixture-level. The test `tests/unit/test_sequential_baselines_ltmle_r.py` compares the
estimate with R `ltmle` 1.3-0 on one panel (witness W9). The test
`tests/unit/test_sequential_baselines_stremr.py` compares it with R `stremr`. Neither test
reports a coverage, a bias or a standard-error ratio, and neither belongs to this study.

## What was tested

| setting | declaration |
| --- | --- |
| estimators | `SequentialGComputation`, and `SequentialIPW` with `normalization="hajek"` and with `"horvitz_thompson"`. Every nuisance is a `GLM` |
| primary laws | `D2` and `G`. `D2` has two nodes, binary covariates and censoring, and a three-arm treatment at node 2. `G` has three nodes, continuous covariates, binary treatment and censoring, and a continuous outcome |
| other laws | `D2B` (binary node 2), `D2S` (absorbing event), `D2C` (two competing causes), `GM` (continuous doses and a modified policy), and `X` (`G` with squared terms that make every main-terms `GLM` wrong) |
| primary estimands | on `D2`, the means of `static_12`, `static_00`, `rule` and `policy`, and the contrast of `static_12` against `static_00`. On `G`, the means of `treat_all`, `treat_none` and `rule`, and their contrast |
| primary size | 1,000 replications of 8,000 rows. The three estimators of one law read the same draw |
| truth | a finite sum over the support for the `D2` family, and a closed form for `G`, `X` and `GM` |
| intervals | pointwise 95% Wald intervals from the influence curve, with `g_bounds = (0.01, 1)` and `q_bounds` at the support of the outcome |
| Monte Carlo inference | 99% intervals around every declared endpoint |

The module `tests/studies/sequential_baselines_laws.py` imports nothing from `cleverly`. Its
truths are an independent statement of the g-formula.

standardized bias inside the margin, the SE-ratio interval inside the calibration band and the
coverage interval inside the coverage band. A control must leave the band on the predicted
side.

| `interval_calibration` | 43 labels at n = 2,000, and `D2C` at n = 8,000, with 8,800 replications each | positive, plus two controls per label: a standard error shrunk by 0.70, and noise added to the estimate | the interval is calibrated when every nuisance is correct. | in sample, without weights or clusters | the study does not cross-fit. The weighted cells `d2w_static_12` cover one weight law. Clusters have fast-tier evidence only |

| a non-saturated binomial first node | the `D2` first-node regression is saturated by the design, so a binomial first node without that saturation has no coverage evidence. The fast-tier witnesses W6 and W10 cover it |
| the fixed-mechanism and first-node cells report | those cells state a predicted direction. They show that the calibration instrument sees a dropped term. The cell `first_node_se` is a property of the law, so it cannot fail for a broken estimator |
| the adaptive cell has one size | the `adaptive_learners` cells fit ICE and Hajek at n = 1,000 with 100 replications, without LTMLE. They report bias in standard deviations to about 0.1 |
| the negative control is the only red set | the three red cells hold the declared misspecification. A misspecification of another kind has no control |
| positivity at small n | `D2C` needs n = 8,000. At n = 2,000 the ICE fit was singular on 227 of 2,000 pilot draws |
