# Fold-evaluated CV-TMLE with baseline strata

This study validates the interval of the fold-evaluated CV-TMLE report when the fit declares
baseline strata. The estimator is `TMLE(cross_fit=True, n_folds=5, cv_evaluation=True,
targeting_scheme="pooled", g_bounds="auto", estimands=("ate", "ey"), simultaneous=False)` fitted
with `strata=["S"]` on complete data, rows as the unit and `repeats=1`. Each stratum estimate is
the $1/V$ average of the stratum plug-ins of the validation folds, each fold's stratum curve
divides by the fold's own stratum share $P_{n,v}(S=s)$, and the marginal estimate is the share
mixture of the stratum estimates with its delta-method curve.
[Fold evaluation with baseline strata](../cv-tmle.md#the-algorithm-as-implemented) gives the
construction and its conditions. Each fit reports 12 parameters: `ate`, `ey[0]` and `ey[1]` in
each of three strata and marginally.

**No canonical implementation is compared.** No pinned comparator reports a fold-averaged
stratified estimate. The `tmle3` CV-TMLE evaluates a pooled mean over the validation predictions,
which is the shipped stacked fit (`cv_evaluation=False`). A zero-row equivalence artifact records
the absence of a comparator.

## What was tested

| setting | declaration |
| --- | --- |
| law | the RM30 family: $W_1 \sim U(-1, 1)$, $W_2 \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.5)$, $\operatorname{logit} g_0(1 \mid W) = 0.3 W_1 - 0.2 W_2$, and mean $m = \bar Q_0(A, W)$ with $\operatorname{logit} \bar Q_0(a, W) = 0.2 + 0.5 W_1 - 0.3 W_2 + a\, b(W_1)$. $S$ cuts $W_1$ at $-0.6$ and $0.1$, so the stratum shares are 0.2, 0.35 and 0.45 |
| bounded outcome | $Y \sim \operatorname{Beta}(12 m, 12 (1 - m))$ with $b = 0.1 + 1.5 W_1$, so the stratum ATEs are $-0.220$, $-0.064$ and $0.184$, fitted with `q_bounds=(0, 1)` |
| binary outcome | $Y \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(m)$ with $b = 0.1 - 0.3 W_1$, so the stratum ATEs are $0.084$, $0.043$ and $-0.016$, fitted with no `q_bounds` |
| settings | `unweighted` and `weighted` on the bounded outcome, `binary_unweighted` and `binary_weighted` on the binary one. The weight $w = 1 + 1.5 W_2$ varies inside every stratum |
| outcome learner | the pairwise products of $(A, W_1, W_2, S)$ with a quasibinomial GLM on the bounded outcome, and with `LogisticRegression(C=1e6, max_iter=5000, tol=1e-10)` on the binary one. Both are correctly specified on the logit scale |
| treatment learner | `LogisticRegression(C=1e6, max_iter=1000)` on $(W_1, W_2, S)$, a correct parametric model |
| truth | fixed: each stratum mean by the trapezoid rule on 4,001 points of $W_1$ per stratum for each $W_2$, weighted by $w(W_2)$. The marginal truth is the share mixture, checked against an adaptive integral to 1e-8 |
| sizes and budgets | primary: n = 1,000 and 2,000 replications for each setting. `interval_calibration`: n = 1,000 and 8,800 replications for each setting. `targeting_necessity`: n = 1,000 and 2,800 replications. `sparse_event_diagnostic`: n = 1,000 and 2,000 replications, with no verdict |
| primary verdicts | the framework defaults: the 99% Student bias interval inside 0.25 SD, the lower end of the 99% Clopper-Pearson coverage interval at or above 0.90, and the SE-ratio interval inside 0.80 to 1.20 |
| seeds | `seed=20263051` and `resampling_seed=20263052` |
| policy | `gated` |

The study has two outcome laws for one reason. The share term of the marginal curve,
$(w_i / \bar w)(\hat\psi_{S_i} - \hat\psi)$, carries about 27% of the marginal `ate` variance under
the beta outcome, whose conditional variance is $m(1 - m)/13$. A fit without it then reads an
asymptotic SE ratio of 0.854 and fails the band. Under the Bernoulli outcome the residual variance
swamps the share term, and the asymptotic ratios without it are 0.999, 0.995 and 0.999 for `ate`,
`ey[0]` and `ey[1]`, above the band floor of 0.93. The binary settings license the binary
outcome, which the support key compares exactly.

The binary blip keeps every stratum-arm's mean between 0.38 and 0.62. At n = 1,000 the smallest
stratum-arm, the treated rows of stratum 0, then holds about 38 rows of the rarer outcome value,
with a 5th percentile of about 30. The binary positives therefore test the construction and not
sparse events, which the descriptive `sparse_event_diagnostic` reads. A first binary pilot with
the bounded blip had about 16 events there and covered `binary_weighted` `ey[1]` in stratum 0 at
0.932, and the stacked fit on the same draws covered it alike. The blip moved before any
registered run, and no band or threshold moved.

`tests/unit/test_fold_evaluated_strata_exact.py` checks the construction on an exact discrete
law: the fold average, the fold-local share, the solved stratum scores and the share term of the
marginal curve. Three deliberate mutations fail it: the whole-sample share in place of the fold's,
no share, and the unstratified fold weights in the update.
`tests/unit/test_fold_evaluated_strata_design.py` computes the truths, the efficiency bounds that
size the noise controls and the asymptotic share-term ratios again, and
`tests/unit/test_fold_evaluated_strata_harness.py` checks the fits, the stratum-code mapping and
the controls.

### What each control shows

The two construction controls show different things, so the page counts them apart: 36 of 36
`unnormalised_curve_control` cells and 4 of 4 `share_dropped_control` cells fall below the band.

`unnormalised_curve_control` fails by construction. Its SE is about the stratum share times the
reported one, and its SE ratios are 0.195 to 0.457 against shares of 0.2 to 0.45. It shows that
the instrument resolves a gross normalisation error. It does not show that the fold-local share
is the right one. The fold-local share against the whole-sample share moves the curve mean by
about $1/n$, which no calibration cell can see, and it rests on the global-share control of
`tests/unit/test_fold_evaluated_strata_exact.py`.

`share_dropped_control` tests the marginal derivation. Without its share term the marginal `ate`
reads SE ratios of 0.8578 rows only and 0.8639 weighted, and `ey[1]` reads 0.6577 and 0.6671,
against the asymptotic 0.854 and 0.656 to 0.660. Its upper endpoints, 0.8813 at the highest, lie
below 0.93. The power comes from the bounded outcome. `ey[0]` carries no such control, because
its share term moves the ratio to about 0.95 only, which 8,800 replications cannot resolve below
the floor.

### What the targeting control shows

The control learner keeps $A$ and $W_2$ of the design $(A, W_1, W_2, S)$, so it is stratum-blind:
its plug-in is nearly the same in every stratum. Its untargeted standardized stratum biases run from
3.59 to 29.5 in size, and the targeted fit's largest is 0.149. The least displaced pair moves by
2.42 positive-arm SDs against the declared 0.5. The cell shows that targeting repairs a
stratum-blind initial fit, a strong test of the fluctuation's stratum blocks. It does not show
that targeting repairs a subtle misspecification within a stratum.

### Sparse events on a binary outcome

On the steeper blip, `ey[1]` in stratum 0 has mean 0.071, and its stratum-arm holds 6.0 rows of
the rarer outcome value on average, with a 5th percentile of 2. There the declared fit covers
0.9090 and the stacked stratified fit on the same draws 0.9155, with SE ratios of 0.9736 and
0.9716. Every other cell of the family covers 0.9390 to 0.9520 for the declared fit. The deficit
is the Wald interval's for a mean near 0.07 with few events: a draw with few events has a low
estimate and a small SE, so the interval misses on the side of the rarer value. The fold-evaluated
fit averages five fold plug-ins with about one such row each and still covers as the stacked fit
does. No verdict reads the family.

## Limitations

| limitation | what it means for use |
| --- | --- |
| There is no cross-implementation evidence | No pinned comparator reports a fold-averaged stratified estimate. The row rests on accuracy against the truth and on the theory properties |
| One size, five folds and one partition | The study fits n = 1,000 with 5 folds and three strata whose smallest share is 0.2, so about 200 rows and about 17 treated rows per stratum-fold. The support key holds neither the fold count nor the shares. With 10 folds at n = 500 about 14% of samples have an empty stratum-fold arm and the fit refuses them with a `DataError`, never a silent estimate. More folds or smaller strata were not measured |
| The arm means and the ATE only | The construction also reports the regime means and their contrasts. No cell measures them, so they read experimental |
| Pointwise intervals only | The study fits `simultaneous=False`. The default simultaneous band of a stratified fit is built and reads experimental |
| `repeats=1` only | With `repeats` above 1 each estimate is the median over draws, and the marginal is the median of the draws' share mixtures. Such a fit reads experimental |
| Rows, complete data and pooled targeting | Clusters, a missing outcome or treatment, an intermediate variable, fold targeting and every other target refuse before any learner, citing [X28](../../roadmap.md#x28-fold-evaluated-cv-tmle-with-baseline-strata) or X61 |
| Correctly specified parametric learners | The outcome and treatment learners contain the truth on the logit scale. A flexible learner rests on the theory alone |
| One known weight | The weighted settings use $w = 1 + 1.5 W_2$, which varies inside every stratum. Estimated weights are not measured |
| The share term is tested on the bounded outcome only | It carries 27% of the marginal `ate` variance there and too little to resolve on the binary outcome. Its formula does not read the outcome type |
| Sparse events on a binary outcome | The binary positives hold about 38 rows of the rarer outcome value in their smallest stratum-arm. With about 6, the declared and the stacked fits both cover about 0.91 in that stratum-arm's mean, and no verdict reads it |
| The unnormalised-curve control resolves a gross error only | It fails by construction. The fold-local share against the whole-sample share rests on the exact-law test |
| The targeting control is stratum-blind | It shows that targeting repairs an initial fit that ignores the strata, not a subtle misspecification within a stratum |
